## SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION AND PURPOSE

This Career Progression Guide is designed to give every SteelCore employee a clear, honest, and actionable view of how careers develop within the organization. It answers the questions that matter most:

- *"What does the path forward look like from my current role?"*
- *"What do I need to learn and demonstrate to advance?"*
- *"How long does it typically take to reach the next level?"*
- *"What are my options if I don't want to manage people?"*

This guide should be read alongside the **Promotion Policy Manual (HR-PROM-002)** and the **Competency Skill Framework (HR-SKF-003)**. Together, these three documents form a complete career development toolkit for every SteelCore employee.

## SECTION 2: CAREER PHILOSOPHY AT STEELCORE

### 2.1 Careers Are Built, Not Assigned

At SteelCore, career advancement is earned through demonstrated performance, proactive skill development, and genuine readiness to take on greater responsibility. No one will build your career for you — but the organization provides every tool, program, and opportunity needed for you to build it yourself.

### 2.2 Both Technical and Leadership Careers Are Valued Equally

SteelCore is a technology-intensive manufacturing organization that requires world-class technical expertise just as much as it requires strong leadership. A **Principal Mechanical Engineer** who has mastered predictive maintenance and reliability engineering contributes as much organizational value as a **Maintenance Manager**. Both career tracks are compensated and recognized equivalently at equivalent grade levels.

### 2.3 Career Progression Is Not Solely Time-Based

Meeting the minimum time-in-grade is a necessary but not sufficient condition for advancement. Employees who perform at the minimum acceptable level will meet time-in-grade requirements but will not be promoted. Promotions require sustained high performance (rating of 4 or 5) and demonstrated competency at the next level.

### 2.4 You Own Your Development

Each employee is responsible for:
- Knowing their current grade and the requirements for the next grade.
- Completing their Individual Development Plan (IDP) in the ESSP each April.
- Actively seeking learning opportunities, stretch assignments, and mentoring.
- Having regular career conversations with their manager.

## SECTION 3: THE TWO CAREER TRACKS

### 3.1 Technical Track

The Technical Track is for employees who find deep technical expertise more rewarding and impactful than people management. It allows exceptional individual contributors to advance to senior grades without taking on formal management responsibilities.

```
Grade 1  — Trainee / Apprentice
   ↓
Grade 2  — Junior Technician / Junior Engineer
   ↓
Grade 3  — Technician / Engineer
   ↓
Grade 4  — Senior Technician / Engineer
   ↓
Grade 5  — Senior Engineer
   ↓
Grade 6  — Lead Engineer / Lead Specialist
   ↓
Grade 7  — Specialist / Principal Engineer          ← Technical Pinnacle for most
   ↓
Grade 7A — Senior Specialist
   ↓
Grade 7B — Principal Specialist
   ↓
Grade 7C — Fellow / Distinguished Engineer          ← Equivalent to Senior Manager
```

### 3.2 Leadership Track

The Leadership Track is for employees who want to lead teams, manage budgets, drive organizational change, and shape the strategic direction of their department.

```
Grade 1  — Trainee / Apprentice
   ↓
Grade 2  — Junior Technician / Junior Engineer
   ↓
Grade 3  — Technician / Engineer
   ↓
Grade 4  — Senior Technician / Engineer
   ↓
Grade 5  — Senior Engineer / Analyst
   ↓
Grade 6  — Lead Engineer / Lead Analyst            ← First opportunity to demonstrate
   ↓                                                  people leadership (project lead)
Grade 8  — Manager / Team Manager                  ← First formal management grade
   ↓
Grade 9  — Senior Manager
   ↓
Grade 10 — Department Head / General Manager
   ↓
Grade 11 — Vice President
   ↓
Grade 12 — Chief Officer (CHRO, CTO, CFO, etc.)
```

> **Track Switching:** Employees may switch from Technical to Leadership track at Grade 5 or 6, provided they demonstrate leadership potential through project leadership, mentoring, and cross-functional work. Track switches must be agreed upon with the HR Business Partner and Department Head.

## SECTION 4: CAREER PROGRESSION — MECHANICAL DEPARTMENT

### 4.1 Progression Map

```
Mechanical Trainee (Grade 1)
├── Duration: 1 year
├── Focus: Safety onboarding, basic tool handling, PM checklist execution
└── Milestone: Complete Safety Induction, basic maintenance tasks independently

        ↓

Junior Mechanical Technician (Grade 2)
├── Duration: 2 years
├── Focus: Preventive maintenance, rotating equipment basics, SAP work orders
└── Milestone: Execute PM independently; complete ME-001 to ME-004

        ↓

Mechanical Technician / Engineer (Grade 3–4)
├── Duration: 2–3 years
├── Focus: Corrective maintenance, FMEA, vibration awareness, hydraulics
└── Milestone: Lead repairs independently; complete ME-005 to ME-008; obtain ISO 18436 Cat I

        ↓

Senior Mechanical Engineer (Grade 5–6)
├── Duration: 3 years
├── Focus: RCM, predictive maintenance, RCA, SAP PM analysis, project lead
└── Milestone: Lead RCA investigations; obtain ISO 18436 Cat II; lead 1+ improvement project

        ↓

Mechanical Specialist / Principal Engineer (Grade 7)
├── Duration: 4 years (for G7A/7B/7C advancement)
├── Focus: Advanced PDM, AI-based maintenance, asset management, knowledge leadership
└── Milestone: CMRP certification; lead departmental PDM program; mentor G3–G5 employees

        ↓

Maintenance Manager (Grade 8) — Leadership Track only
├── Duration: 4+ years
├── Focus: Team leadership, maintenance KPIs, budget management, strategic maintenance planning
└── Milestone: First-Time Manager Program; manage team of 8+ technicians
```

### 4.2 Key Decision Points — Mechanical

| Grade | Decision Point | Options |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 6 | Continue Technical vs. move to Leadership | Technical: advance to Specialist; Leadership: apply for project/team lead roles |
| Grade 7 | Deepen specialization vs. management | Technical: Senior/Principal Specialist; Leadership: nominate for Manager grade |

### 4.3 Milestone Certifications — Mechanical

| Grade Band | Key Certification |
|---|---|
| G3–G5 | ISO 18436 Category II Vibration Analyst |
| G6–G7 | CMRP (SMRP), ISO 55001 Asset Management |
| G8 | PMP or equivalent; ISO 55001 Lead Auditor |

## SECTION 14: CROSS-FUNCTIONAL MOBILITY

### 14.1 Internal Transfer Policy

SteelCore encourages cross-functional experience as a key enabler of career development. Employees may apply for internal transfers after a minimum of **2 years in their current role** during open transfer windows (May and November).

Common and encouraged cross-functional moves:

| From | To | Career Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical Engineering | Maintenance | Broader asset management perspective |
| Automation | AI & Digital | Digital manufacturing specialization |
| Operations | Quality | Process-quality integration expertise |
| Data Analytics | AI & Digital | AI/ML specialization pathway |
| Any Technical | HR (L&D) | Technical training facilitation and L&D design |
| Any Technical | IT | IT/OT convergence specialization |

### 14.2 High Potential Rotations

Employees in the High Potential (HiPo) program complete two 3-month cross-functional rotations as part of their development. Rotations are assigned by the HR team in consultation with the employee and both department heads.

## SECTION 15: CAREER CONVERSATION GUIDE FOR EMPLOYEES

Use the following questions to prepare for your career conversation with your manager:

**Where am I now?**
- What grade am I at, and how long have I been in this grade?
- What is my current competency level vs. the requirement for my grade?
- What was my last performance rating?

**Where do I want to go?**
- Do I want to pursue the Technical Track or Leadership Track?
- What is my target role in 3 years? In 5 years?
- What kind of work energizes me most?

**What's the gap?**
- Which competencies do I need to develop to reach the next grade?
- What certifications do I need to obtain?
- What experiences (projects, rotations, leadership opportunities) would accelerate my development?

**What's my plan?**
- What specific learning programs will I enroll in this year?
- What project or stretch assignment can I request?
- Who can mentor me in areas where I need development?

## SECTION 16: CAREER CONVERSATION GUIDE FOR MANAGERS

As a manager, you are the most important enabler of your team member's career growth. Use these guidelines for effective career conversations:

**Prepare:**
- Review the employee's IDP, competency assessment, and performance history before the meeting.
- Identify 2–3 specific growth areas and 2–3 genuine strengths to discuss.

**Listen First:**
- Ask open questions: *"Where do you see yourself in 3 years? What aspects of your work do you find most energizing?"*
- Understand whether the employee is interested in the Technical or Leadership track.

**Be Honest:**
- Share your genuine assessment of the employee's readiness for the next grade.
- If there are gaps, be specific and constructive.

**Co-Create the Plan:**
- Agree on 2–3 development priorities for the year.
- Identify specific learning programs, projects, or mentoring relationships.
- Commit to a stretch assignment that provides real career-advancing experience.

**Follow Up:**
- Career conversations should happen at least **twice per year** — not only during appraisal season.
- Track IDP progress quarterly and offer course corrections early.

## SECTION 17: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

**Q1: I have been in my grade for the minimum time. Does that mean I will be promoted?**
> No. Meeting the minimum time-in-grade is necessary but not sufficient. You also need a performance rating at or above the threshold, a passing competency assessment, required certifications, and a manager nomination. Promotion requires all criteria to be met simultaneously.

**Q2: I am in the Technical Track at Grade 7. Can I still earn a salary comparable to a Manager?**
> Yes. Grade 7A (Senior Specialist), 7B (Principal Specialist), and 7C (Fellow/Distinguished Engineer) are compensated at bands equivalent to Grade 8–9 on the Leadership Track. Technical excellence is valued and rewarded equally.

**Q3: How do I find a mentor within the company?**
> Log in to the ESSP → My Learning → Mentoring Program. You can browse available mentors by department and grade. You may also approach a senior colleague directly. The L&D team can facilitate mentor matching if needed.

**Q4: I want to move from Mechanical Engineering to AI & Digital Transformation. Is that possible?**
> Yes, with a structured plan. You would typically complete the AI Literacy program, then the Data Analytics Learning Path (DS-003 to DS-008), and eventually the AI Engineering Path. A cross-functional transfer after 2 years in your current role is supported. Speak with your HRBP to map the transition plan.

**Q5: My manager has not had a career conversation with me in over a year. What should I do?**
> You have the right to request a career conversation at any time. Send your manager a meeting request specifically for an IDP review. If this is not addressed, escalate to your HR Business Partner. Career conversations are a manager's mandatory responsibility.

**Q6: What is the fastest realistic timeline to go from Grade 3 to Grade 7?**
> The minimum timeline is: 2 years (G3→G4) + 2 years (G4→G5) + 3 years (G5→G6) + 3 years (G6→G7) = 10 years minimum. With consistently exceptional performance, required certifications, and accelerated promotion exceptions, the fastest realistic timeline is approximately 7–8 years. These timelines assume a performance rating of 4–5 maintained throughout.

**Q7: Can I be promoted if my department has no vacancy at the next grade?**
> Promotions require headcount approval from Finance and the Business Head. If there is no vacancy, promotion may be deferred until a position is available or created. However, employees who are approved for promotion but deferred due to headcount constraints will receive a merit increment as recognition and will be prioritized in the next promotion cycle.
