## SECTION 1: SAFETY POLICY STATEMENT

SteelCore Industries Limited is absolutely committed to maintaining the highest standards of Safety, Health, and Environment (SHE) across all operations. The safety and wellbeing of every employee, contractor, and visitor is the organization's foremost priority — superseding all production targets, schedule pressures, and cost objectives.

**Our Safety Commitment:**

- Zero tolerance for unsafe acts and unsafe conditions.
- Every employee has the right and the responsibility to stop unsafe work — without fear of reprisal.
- Management at all levels is personally accountable for safety performance in their area of control.
- Safety is not a department — it is everyone's responsibility, every moment of every shift.

**Managing Director's Statement:**
> *"At SteelCore, we believe that all accidents are preventable. No order, no production target, and no deadline is worth a single injury. I expect every leader, manager, and employee to uphold our Life-Saving Rules without exception."*

This Safety Manual is mandatory reading for all SteelCore employees, contractors, and authorized visitors. Compliance is a condition of entry to all SteelCore facilities.

## SECTION 2: LIFE-SAVING RULES (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

The following **12 Life-Saving Rules** are absolute and non-negotiable. Violation of any Life-Saving Rule — by any employee at any grade — will result in immediate suspension and a disciplinary process that may lead to termination:

| # | Life-Saving Rule | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **LSR-01** | **Work with a Valid Permit** | No work without a valid Work Permit for all non-routine tasks. No exceptions. |
| **LSR-02** | **Lockout/Tagout (LOTO)** | All energy sources must be isolated and verified zero-energy before maintenance work begins. |
| **LSR-03** | **No Mobile Phones in Production Areas** | Mobile phones are prohibited in all production, maintenance, and high-risk areas. |
| **LSR-04** | **PPE Compliance** | Mandatory PPE must be worn at all times in designated areas. No exceptions. |
| **LSR-05** | **No Bypass of Safety Systems** | Safety interlocks, guards, and emergency stops must never be bypassed, defeated, or overridden. |
| **LSR-06** | **Confined Space Permit** | No entry into a confined space without a valid Confined Space Entry Permit and gas testing. |
| **LSR-07** | **Working at Heights Harness** | A full-body safety harness must be worn and anchored at all heights above 1.8 meters. |
| **LSR-08** | **No Driving Under Influence** | Operating any vehicle or mobile equipment while impaired by alcohol, drugs, or fatigue is prohibited. |
| **LSR-09** | **Hot Work Permit** | All welding, grinding, cutting, and open-flame work requires a Hot Work Permit and fire watch. |
| **LSR-10** | **Safe Lifting Practices** | Suspended loads must never be walked under. Pre-use crane and sling checks are mandatory. |
| **LSR-11** | **Report All Incidents** | All incidents, near-misses, and unsafe conditions must be reported within 1 hour. No exceptions. |
| **LSR-12** | **Right to Refuse Unsafe Work** | Any employee may refuse work they believe is unsafe. This right is protected and cannot be penalized. |

## SECTION 4: MACHINE SAFETY AND GUARDING

### 4.1 Machine Guarding Principles

All rotating machinery, nip points, shear points, and pinch points in SteelCore plants are fitted with machine guards per IS:11016 and the Factories Act 1948. The following principles apply:

- **No guard, no operation:** Machinery with missing, damaged, or bypassed guards must be immediately shut down and tagged out. It must not be restarted until the guard is repaired or replaced.
- Guards may only be removed for maintenance activities and must be replaced before the equipment is energized.
- Temporary guards during maintenance must meet the same protection standard as permanent guards.

### 4.2 Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) Procedure

LOTO is mandatory before any maintenance, repair, inspection, or cleaning of machinery where unexpected energization or startup could cause injury.

**SteelCore Standard LOTO Procedure (6 Steps):**

1. **Notify:** Inform all affected personnel that LOTO will be applied.
2. **Identify:** Identify all energy sources (electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic, gravity, thermal, chemical).
3. **Isolate:** Apply isolation at each identified energy source using the designated isolation point.
4. **Lock:** Each technician applies their personal lock to the isolation device.
5. **Tag:** Apply a danger tag with name, date, and contact details.
6. **Verify:** Test that the equipment cannot be energized. Release stored energy (drain pressure, block suspended loads).

**LOTO Removal:** Locks are removed only by the individual who applied them. Group LOTO procedures apply when multiple trades work on the same equipment.

### 4.3 Pre-Use Equipment Checks

Before operating any plant equipment, process machinery, or mobile equipment, the operator must complete a Pre-Use Inspection Checklist, including:

- Visual inspection of guards, safety devices, and control systems.
- Check for fluid leaks, unusual noise, or vibration.
- Verify emergency stop function.
- Confirm the area is clear of personnel.

## SECTION 5: ELECTRICAL SAFETY

### 5.1 Electrical Safety Rules

- Only **Competent Authorized Persons** may work on or near live electrical equipment. Authorization is issued by the Chief Electrical Engineer and is grade/training-specific.
- Working on live electrical systems is prohibited except where de-energization creates a greater hazard (requires written authorization from the Head of Electrical Engineering).
- **Safe approach distances** must be maintained at all times:
  - LV systems (up to 1kV): 300mm minimum approach distance.
  - MV systems (1kV–33kV): Requires full arc flash PPE and permit.
  - HV systems (above 33kV): Requires switching crew procedure and SHE witness.

### 5.2 Arc Flash Safety

Arc flash is one of the most severe electrical hazards in steel plant environments. SteelCore's arc flash safety requirements:

- All electrical panels and switchboards are labeled with arc flash incident energy (cal/cm²) and required PPE category per NFPA 70E.
- Arc flash PPE must match or exceed the labeled incident energy.
- No work on or near energized electrical equipment without the correct arc flash PPE.

### 5.3 Electrical Permit to Work

All work on electrical systems — including testing, inspection, isolation, and maintenance — requires a completed Electrical Permit to Work (E-PTW), issued by the Shift Electrical Engineer. The E-PTW must specify:
- System to be worked on.
- Method of isolation.
- PPE required.
- Arc flash hazard category.
- Authorized persons on the permit.

## SECTION 6: FIRE SAFETY AND PREVENTION

### 6.1 Fire Prevention Rules

- No smoking except in designated smoking zones.
- Flammable and combustible materials must be stored in approved, clearly labeled, ventilated storage areas.
- Hot work (welding, cutting, grinding) is prohibited within 15 meters of flammable storage without special authorization.
- Combustible waste must be cleared from work areas at the end of every shift.
- All fire exits, fire hydrants, and fire extinguishers must be kept clear and unobstructed at all times.

### 6.2 Fire Extinguisher Types and Use

| Extinguisher Type | Color Code | Use For | Do NOT Use For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water | Red | Class A fires (wood, paper, fabric) | Electrical, flammable liquids, metals |
| CO2 (Carbon Dioxide) | Black | Electrical fires, Class B (flammable liquids) | Class A, Class D (metal fires) |
| Dry Powder (ABC) | Blue | Class A, B, C — multi-purpose | Electrical (leaves residue), precision equipment |
| Foam (AFFF) | Cream | Class B (flammable liquids), Class A | Electrical fires, metal fires |
| Wet Chemical | Yellow | Class F (cooking oils, fats) | Electrical, Class D |
| Dry Sand (Class D) | None | Metal fires (magnesium, aluminum) | All other classes |

**PASS Technique:** Pull the pin → Aim at the base of the fire → Squeeze the handle → Sweep side to side.

### 6.3 Emergency Evacuation Procedure

1. On discovering a fire or hearing the fire alarm, activate the nearest fire alarm break-glass immediately.
2. Call SHE Control Room: Ext. 999.
3. Evacuate the building or area immediately via the nearest designated fire exit. Do not use elevators.
4. Do not stop to collect personal belongings.
5. Proceed to the designated Assembly Point for your building/area (assembly points are marked on all floor plans and site maps).
6. The Shift Safety Officer will conduct a headcount and confirm all persons accounted for.
7. Do not re-enter the building until the All Clear signal is given by the Fire Incident Commander.

### 6.4 Hot Work Permit Procedure

Hot work (welding, cutting, grinding, brazing) generates sparks and heat that can ignite fires. All hot work outside of designated welding bays requires:

1. A valid Hot Work Permit, issued by the Shift Safety Officer.
2. A fire watch — a dedicated person stationed at the work site throughout the work and for 30 minutes after work is complete.
3. Fire extinguisher available and tested at the work site.
4. All flammable materials removed or shielded within a 15-meter radius.

## SECTION 7: CONFINED SPACE ENTRY

### 7.1 Definition

A confined space is defined as an enclosed or partially enclosed space that is not designed for continuous human occupancy, has limited or restricted means of entry or exit, and may present atmospheric, engulfment, or physical entrapment hazards.

**Examples at SteelCore:** Blast furnace internals (during cold repair), stoves, gas mains, sumps, tanks, vessel interiors, sewers, pits, trenches.

### 7.2 Confined Space Hazards

| Hazard | Example | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen Deficiency | Displacement by CO, CO2, N2 | Unconsciousness, death in minutes |
| Toxic Atmosphere | Carbon Monoxide (CO) from blast furnace | Poisoning, death |
| Flammable Atmosphere | Coke oven gas, LPG | Explosion, fire |
| Physical Entrapment | Materials or structure collapse | Crushing, entrapment |
| Engulfment | Sudden influx of material or liquid | Drowning, suffocation |
| Extreme Temperature | Hot surfaces, steam | Burns, heat stress |

### 7.3 Confined Space Entry Procedure

**Step 1 — Pre-Entry:**
- Obtain a Confined Space Entry Permit from the Shift Safety Officer.
- Conduct gas testing for Oxygen (19.5–23.5%), CO, H2S, and LEL (flammable gas) using a calibrated multi-gas detector.
- All energy sources must be isolated and LOTO applied.
- Establish communication protocol between entrant and attendant.

**Step 2 — Entry:**
- At least one Standby Attendant must remain outside the confined space at all times during entry.
- Entrant must wear full body harness with rescue line attached.
- Emergency escape breathing apparatus (EEBA) or SCBA must be available at the point of entry.
- Continuous gas monitoring during entry using a personal gas detector.

**Step 3 — Rescue:**
- If the entrant becomes incapacitated, the attendant initiates non-entry rescue using the harness and retrieval system.
- SHE Control Room (Ext. 999) and Emergency Response Team (ERT) are called immediately.
- Entry rescue by the ERT is conducted only with SCBA.

### 7.4 Atmospheric Limits for Entry

| Gas / Parameter | Safe Entry Limit |
|---|---|
| Oxygen | 19.5% to 23.5% |
| Carbon Monoxide (CO) | Below 25 ppm |
| Hydrogen Sulfide (H2S) | Below 10 ppm |
| Flammable Gas (LEL) | Below 10% of LEL |

## SECTION 8: WORKING AT HEIGHTS

### 8.1 Definition

Work at heights is defined as any work activity where a person could fall from a level above 1.8 meters. This includes work on rooftops, elevated platforms, ladders, scaffolding, mobile elevated work platforms (MEWPs), and structural steelwork.

### 8.2 Fall Prevention Hierarchy

| Priority | Control Measure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Highest) | Eliminate the need to work at height | Design maintenance access at ground level |
| 2 | Collective fall prevention | Edge protection, toe boards, guardrails |
| 3 | Collective fall arrest | Safety nets, soft landing systems |
| 4 | Individual fall prevention | Fall restraint system — prevents reaching the fall edge |
| 5 | Individual fall arrest | Full-body harness with energy absorber — arrests a fall |

### 8.3 Equipment Requirements

- **Full-Body Harness:** EN 361 compliant, inspected pre-use.
- **Lanyard with Energy Absorber:** Maximum free-fall distance 1.5 meters.
- **Anchor Point:** Rated minimum 15 kN (approximately 1,500 kg) for a single user.
- **Scaffold:** Erected and certified by a competent scaffolder. Inspection tag must be displayed (Green: Safe to Use, Yellow: Use with Restrictions, Red: Do Not Use).
- **MEWP (Aerial Work Platform):** Operators must hold a valid MEWP operator certificate. Secondary guarding (gate secured) required at all times during elevated work.
- **Ladders:** Only for access (not as a work platform), 3-point contact required at all times, secured at top and bottom.

### 8.4 Working at Heights Permit

A Working at Heights Permit is required for all work above 1.8 meters outside of permanently installed guardrailed platforms. The permit must be obtained from the Shift Safety Officer before work commences.

## SECTION 9: HOT METAL AND MOLTEN MATERIAL SAFETY

### 9.1 Overview

SteelCore's Blast Furnace, Steelmaking, and Casting operations involve hot metal at temperatures exceeding 1,400°C and molten steel at temperatures above 1,500°C. Exposure to these materials is an extreme hazard requiring specialized PPE and strict procedural compliance.

### 9.2 Mandatory PPE for Hot Metal Areas

- **Aluminized face shield** with full-face coverage (minimum 8 cal/cm² arc flash rating equivalent thermal protection).
- **Aluminized proximity suit** (Type C) for work within 2 meters of molten metal tapping, teeming, or casting operations.
- **Aluminized gauntlet gloves** — minimum Grade 3 thermal resistance.
- **Metatarsal safety boots** with heat-resistant soles (HRO rated per EN ISO 20345).

### 9.3 Hot Metal Safety Rules

- **No water near hot metal or molten steel zones.** Even a small quantity of water in contact with hot metal causes a steam explosion. Water-cooled equipment must be inspected for leaks before and after each heat.
- All personnel in the casting pit, taphole area, and ladle treatment stations during active operations must stand in designated safe zones marked on the floor.
- No unauthorized personnel within the defined Hot Metal Exclusion Zone during tapping, teeming, or casting.
- Equipment used in hot metal contact (ladles, runners, troughs) must be properly dried and preheated before use.

## SECTION 10: CHEMICAL AND HAZARDOUS MATERIAL SAFETY

### 10.1 Hazardous Materials at SteelCore

Key hazardous materials encountered at SteelCore facilities include:

| Material | Location | Primary Hazard |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon Monoxide (CO) | Blast Furnace, Coke Oven Gas System | Asphyxiation, poisoning |
| Sulfuric Acid | Pickling Lines | Chemical burns, inhalation |
| Hydrochloric Acid | Pickling, Surface Treatment | Chemical burns, toxic vapor |
| Zinc (molten) | Galvanizing Lines | Metal fume fever, explosion with water |
| Lime / Quicklime | Steelmaking | Burns (contact + inhalation) |
| Mineral Oils and Lubricants | Maintenance areas | Skin sensitization, fire hazard |
| Asbestos (legacy) | Older insulation (pre-2010 areas) | Mesothelioma, lung cancer |

### 10.2 Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS / SDS)

Safety Data Sheets (SDS) for all hazardous chemicals used at SteelCore are available in:
- The SHE document management system (accessible via ESSP).
- Physical binder at each area chemical store.

Employees handling hazardous chemicals must read and understand the SDS before handling the material for the first time.

### 10.3 Chemical Spill Response

**Small Spill (< 20 liters):**
1. Alert nearby personnel.
2. Apply appropriate absorbent material (sand, vermiculite, or proprietary absorbent).
3. Collect and dispose as hazardous waste.
4. Report to area supervisor within 1 hour.

**Large Spill (> 20 liters or unknown material):**
1. Evacuate the area immediately.
2. Call SHE Control Room: Ext. 999.
3. Activate the Spill Response Team (SRT).
4. Do not attempt to clean up without proper PPE and SRT supervision.

## SECTION 11: EMERGENCY RESPONSE PROCEDURES

### 11.1 Emergency Categories

| Category | Description | Response Level |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 — Local Emergency | Incident contained within a work area (minor injury, small fire, small spill) | Area Supervisor + Shift Safety Officer |
| Level 2 — Site Emergency | Incident affecting multiple areas or significant injury/property damage | Emergency Response Team (ERT) + Plant Management |
| Level 3 — Major Emergency | Major fire, explosion, mass casualty event, or off-site impact | Full Emergency Response + External Services (Fire Brigade, Ambulance, Police) |

### 11.2 Emergency Response Team (ERT)

The ERT operates 24/7 with a minimum team of 8 trained responders per shift. The ERT is trained in:
- First aid and CPR (certified).
- Fire fighting (trained firefighters).
- Confined space rescue.
- Chemical spill response.
- High-angle rescue (working at heights rescue).

### 11.3 Emergency Signal System

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Continuous siren (3 seconds on, 3 seconds off) | Fire / Evacuation alert — evacuate immediately |
| Continuous siren (long blast) | All Clear — safe to return |
| PA announcement: "Code Blue" | Medical emergency — ERT responding |
| PA announcement: "Code Red" | Fire / Major emergency |
| PA announcement: "Code Yellow" | Security incident |

### 11.4 First Aid Response

First Aid Kits are located at all production areas, administrative offices, and gate entry points. Locations are marked on area safety maps.

Trained First Aiders are present on every shift. The Shift Safety Officer maintains the First Aider roster.

**For a serious injury or medical emergency:**
1. Ensure the area is safe before approaching.
2. Call SHE Control Room: Ext. 999 immediately.
3. Do not move the injured person unless they are in immediate danger.
4. Apply first aid within your level of training until the ERT arrives.
5. Complete an Incident Report Form within 4 hours.

## SECTION 12: INCIDENT REPORTING AND INVESTIGATION

### 12.1 Incident Classification

| Category | Description | Reporting Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Near Miss | An event that could have caused injury or damage but did not | Within 1 hour to supervisor; formal report within 24 hours |
| First Aid Injury | Minor injury treated at the first aid station, no lost time | Within 1 hour; formal report within 24 hours |
| Medical Treatment Injury | Injury requiring medical treatment beyond first aid | Within 1 hour; formal report within 24 hours |
| Lost Time Injury (LTI) | Injury resulting in absence from work beyond the shift of injury | Immediate notification to SHE Manager and Plant Head |
| Serious Injury | Fracture, hospitalization, permanent disability | Immediate notification to SHE Manager, MD, and statutory authorities |
| Fatality | Loss of life | Immediate notification to MD, CHRO, local authorities, statutory bodies |

### 12.2 Reporting Procedure

1. Notify the area supervisor immediately.
2. Notify the Shift Safety Officer.
3. Call SHE Control Room: Ext. 999.
4. Secure the scene — do not disturb evidence except to prevent further harm.
5. Complete the Online Incident Report Form (ESSP → SHE Module) within the specified deadline.

### 12.3 Investigation Process

All incidents are investigated by a cross-functional team:

**First Aid / Near Miss:** Investigated by the area supervisor + Shift Safety Officer within 24 hours.
**Medical Treatment Injury / LTI:** Investigated by Department Head + SHE Manager within 48 hours.
**Serious Injury / Fatality:** Investigated by a multi-disciplinary team led by the SHE Director within 72 hours. Investigation report submitted to statutory authorities within the legally required period.

**Root Cause Analysis methodology:** All investigations use the Fault Tree Analysis (FTA) or Cause-and-Effect (5-Why) technique to identify root causes.

### 12.4 Corrective Actions

All investigations must produce SMART corrective actions:
- Specifically describing what will be changed (engineering, process, training, or behavioral).
- Assigned to a responsible person.
- With a defined completion date.
- Verified as effective (not just completed) within 90 days.

## SECTION 13: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION AND RISK ASSESSMENT

### 13.1 Hazard Identification (HIRA)

Every job task performed at SteelCore must be preceded by a Hazard Identification and Risk Assessment (HIRA) for non-routine, complex, or first-time activities. For routine activities, the department's standing HIRA documents apply.

**HIRA Steps:**

1. **Identify Hazards:** List all hazards associated with each step of the task (physical, chemical, electrical, ergonomic, behavioral).
2. **Assess Risk:** Evaluate the probability and consequence of each hazard using the Risk Matrix.
3. **Implement Controls:** Apply controls in hierarchy order (eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, PPE).
4. **Verify:** Confirm controls are effective and in place before work commences.
5. **Review:** HIRA must be reviewed after any incident related to the task or if the task changes significantly.

### 13.2 Risk Matrix

| Likelihood / Consequence | Negligible (1) | Minor (2) | Moderate (3) | Major (4) | Catastrophic (5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rare (1)** | Low | Low | Low | Medium | High |
| **Unlikely (2)** | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | High |
| **Possible (3)** | Low | Medium | Medium | High | Critical |
| **Likely (4)** | Medium | Medium | High | Critical | Critical |
| **Almost Certain (5)** | Medium | High | Critical | Critical | Critical |

| Risk Level | Action Required |
|---|---|
| Low | Manage by routine procedures |
| Medium | Manage by specific procedures, monitor |
| High | Senior management approval required; additional controls mandatory |
| Critical | Stop work immediately; escalate to SHE Director; work may not resume without written authorization |

## SECTION 14: SAFETY TRAINING REQUIREMENTS

### 14.1 Induction Training (All New Joiners and Contractors)

All new employees and contractors must complete a **16-hour Safety Induction Program** before entering any operational area. The induction covers:

- Site safety rules and Life-Saving Rules.
- Emergency response procedures and alarm signals.
- Hazard identification.
- PPE requirements and usage.
- Incident reporting.
- Site-specific hazards (blast furnace gas, high temperature, electrical, working at heights).

Induction is conducted by the SHE Department. A competency test with a minimum pass score of 80% must be completed and recorded before the employee/contractor is issued a site access permit.

### 14.2 Annual Safety Refresher Training

All employees must complete a minimum of **16 hours of safety training per year**, including:

- 8-hour General Safety Refresher.
- 4-hour Fire Safety and Emergency Response.
- Role-specific safety training (LOTO, confined space, working at heights, arc flash, hot metal, as applicable).

### 14.3 Specialist Safety Certifications

| First Aider Certification | Minimum 1 per 25 employees per shift | Every 2 years |
